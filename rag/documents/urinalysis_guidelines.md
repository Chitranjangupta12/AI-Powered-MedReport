# Routine Urinalysis Reference Guidelines

**Source Organization**: National Institutes of Health (NIH MedlinePlus)
**URL**: https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/urinalysis/
**Category**: Nephrology and Urology
**Last Reviewed**: 2024

## Overview
A routine urinalysis is a diagnostic physical, chemical, and microscopic examination of urine. It serves to detect metabolic conditions, renal parenchymal diseases, and urinary tract infections.

## Key Parameters

### 1. Leukocyte Esterase and Nitrites
- **Significance**: Leukocyte esterase indicates the presence of white blood cells (pyuria). Nitrites are produced by Gram-negative bacteria (such as E. coli) that reduce dietary nitrate.
- **Normal**: Negative.
- **Positive Findings**: Highly suggestive of a urinary tract infection (UTI). When accompanied by dysuria, frequency, pelvic pain, or systemic symptoms like fever and chills, prompt medical evaluation and potential urine culture are recommended.

### 2. Urine Protein (Albumin)
- **Normal**: Negative or Trace (< 15 mg/dL).
- **Abnormal (1+ to 4+ or persistent proteinuria)**: Transient benign proteinuria can occur with strenuous exercise, dehydration, orthostatic changes, or acute fever. Persistent proteinuria is an early hallmark of glomerular barrier disruption (diabetic nephropathy, glomerulonephritis) and warrants nephrological follow-up.

### 3. Blood / Red Blood Cells (Hematuria)
- **Normal**: Negative or < 3 RBCs per high power field.
- **Abnormal (Microscopic or Gross Hematuria)**: May stem from benign causes (vigorous exercise, contamination during menstruation) or pathology including nephrolithiasis (kidney stones), infection, trauma, or urothelial neoplasms. Must be discussed with a healthcare provider for formal evaluation.

### 4. Urine Glucose & Ketones
- **Normal**: Negative.
- **Positive**: Glucosuria occurs when serum glucose exceeds renal threshold (~180 mg/dL). Ketonuria signals rapid fat breakdown, seen in diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA), prolonged fasting, or extreme ketogenic dieting.
