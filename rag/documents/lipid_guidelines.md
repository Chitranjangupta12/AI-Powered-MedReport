# Lipid Panel & Cardiovascular Health Guidelines

**Source Organization**: American Heart Association (AHA) & Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)
**URL**: https://www.cdc.gov/cholesterol/about.html
**Category**: Cardiovascular & Lipidology
**Last Reviewed**: 2024

## Overview
A lipid panel (or lipid profile) assesses circulating concentrations of lipids and lipoproteins. It serves as an essential tool for evaluating long-term atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) risk.

## Key Parameters

### 1. Total Cholesterol
- **Desirable**: < 200 mg/dL
- **Borderline High**: 200 - 239 mg/dL
- **High**: >= 240 mg/dL

### 2. Low-Density Lipoprotein (LDL) Cholesterol
- **Role**: Often termed "bad cholesterol" because particles infiltrate endothelial walls, oxidize, and contribute to atherosclerotic plaque formation.
- **Optimal**: < 100 mg/dL (or < 70 mg/dL for patients with established cardiovascular disease)
- **Near/Above Optimal**: 100 - 129 mg/dL
- **Borderline High**: 130 - 159 mg/dL
- **High**: 160 - 189 mg/dL
- **Very High**: >= 190 mg/dL
- **Clinical Significance**: Elevated LDL represents a modifiable cardiovascular risk factor. Initial management typically emphasizes dietary adjustments (reducing saturated/trans fats), regular aerobic exercise, and smoking cessation. If risk remains elevated, clinicians evaluate statin therapy based on overall 10-year cardiovascular risk calculators.

### 3. High-Density Lipoprotein (HDL) Cholesterol
- **Role**: Termed "good cholesterol" for mediating reverse cholesterol transport, shuttling excess cholesterol from peripheral arteries back to the liver for hepatic clearance.
- **Protective Levels**: >= 60 mg/dL
- **Higher Risk / Low Levels**: < 40 mg/dL (men), < 50 mg/dL (women)

### 4. Triglycerides
- **Normal**: < 150 mg/dL
- **Borderline High**: 150 - 199 mg/dL
- **High**: 200 - 499 mg/dL
- **Very High (Critical Alert)**: >= 500 mg/dL (increases the acute risk of acute pancreatitis and warrants prompt clinical review).
