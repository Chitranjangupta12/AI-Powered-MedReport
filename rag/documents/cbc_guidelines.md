# Complete Blood Count (CBC) Reference Guide

**Source Organization**: National Institutes of Health (NIH) - MedlinePlus & National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute (NHLBI)
**URL**: https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/complete-blood-count/
**Category**: Hematology
**Last Reviewed**: 2024

## Overview
The Complete Blood Count (CBC) is one of the most widely performed routine laboratory blood tests. It measures the quantity, size, and maturity of cellular elements circulating within peripheral blood.

## Key Parameters and Clinical Context

### 1. Hemoglobin (Hb)
- **Biological Function**: Iron-rich protein contained within Red Blood Cells (RBCs) responsible for binding and delivering oxygen from lungs to systemic tissues.
- **Typical Adult Reference Range**:
  - Males: 13.8 - 17.2 g/dL
  - Females: 12.1 - 15.1 g/dL
  *(Note: Specific laboratory reference ranges may vary slightly based on analyzer methodology, altitude, and demographic cohorts).*
- **Low Levels (Anemia)**: May arise from iron deficiency, acute or occult blood loss, vitamin B12 or folate deficiency, chronic inflammatory states, or bone marrow disorders. Common clinical signs include fatigue, dyspnea on exertion, lightheadedness, and pallor.
- **Critical/Panic Thresholds**: Hemoglobin levels below 7.0 g/dL often require prompt hospital or emergency medical evaluation due to compromised systemic oxygenation.

### 2. White Blood Cell Count (WBC / Leukocytes)
- **Biological Function**: Primary defensive cells of the innate and adaptive immune system.
- **Typical Adult Reference Range**: 4,000 - 11,000 /uL (or 4.0 - 11.0 x 10^9 /L).
- **Elevated Levels (Leukocytosis)**: Most frequently an expected physiological reaction to bacterial or viral infections, systemic inflammation, severe physical exertion, smoking, or tissue trauma. Marked elevations (>30,000 /uL) require prompt hematological evaluation.
- **Depressed Levels (Leukopenia)**: May occur with viral infections, autoimmune conditions, medications (e.g., chemotherapy, immunosuppressants), or bone marrow disorders.

### 3. Platelets (Thrombocytes)
- **Biological Function**: Cytoplasmic fragments originating from megakaryocytes that aggregate to form primary hemostatic plugs upon vascular endothelial injury.
- **Typical Adult Reference Range**: 150,000 - 450,000 /uL.
- **Low Levels (Thrombocytopenia)**: Elevated risk of mucosal bleeding, spontaneous petechiae, purpura, or epistaxis. Levels under 50,000 /uL warrant urgent clinical attention.
- **High Levels (Thrombocytosis)**: Often reactive/secondary to iron deficiency, acute infection, or chronic inflammation.

## Important Clinical Disclaimers
Laboratories calibrate reference intervals using specific population statistics. Abnormal values on a CBC must always be interpreted in the context of the patient's comprehensive clinical history, physical examination, and concurrent medications.
