# Comprehensive Metabolic Panel: Liver and Kidney Function Guidelines

**Source Organization**: National Institutes of Health (NIH MedlinePlus) & National Kidney Foundation (NKF)
**URL**: https://www.kidney.org/atoz/content/understanding-your-lab-values
**Category**: Hepatology and Nephrology
**Last Reviewed**: 2024

## Liver Function Tests (LFT)

### Alanine Aminotransferase (ALT) and Aspartate Aminotransferase (AST)
- **Biological Role**: Intracellular enzymes involved in amino acid metabolism. ALT is predominantly concentrated in hepatocytes; AST is found in liver, cardiac muscle, skeletal muscle, and kidneys.
- **Typical Reference Range**: ALT: 7 - 56 U/L; AST: 10 - 40 U/L (lab specific).
- **Elevated Values**:
  - *Mild-to-moderate elevation (2x to 5x upper limit)*: Frequently indicates nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD/MASLD), chronic medication effects (e.g., statins, acetaminophen, NSAIDs), moderate alcohol consumption, or chronic viral hepatitis.
  - *Marked elevation (>10x to 20x upper limit)*: Signals acute hepatocellular necrosis, acute viral hepatitis, ischemic hepatitis ("shock liver"), or toxin-induced injury. Prompt medical evaluation is required.

### Alkaline Phosphatase (ALP) & Total Bilirubin
- **ALP**: Found in biliary duct epithelium and bone. Elevation with normal ALT/AST may reflect biliary obstruction, cholestasis, or bone turnover.
- **Bilirubin**: Breakdown product of heme. Hyperbilirubinemia may cause jaundice (yellowing of sclera/skin), tea-colored urine, or acholic stools.

## Kidney Function Tests (KFT / Renal Panel)

### Serum Creatinine
- **Biological Role**: Metabolic byproduct of muscle creatine breakdown. Excreted almost entirely via glomerular filtration without substantial tubular reabsorption.
- **Typical Reference Range**: 0.6 - 1.2 mg/dL (varies by age, sex, and muscular body surface area).
- **Elevated Creatinine**: Reflects diminished glomerular filtration rate (GFR). Acute increases can occur secondary to severe dehydration (prerenal azotemia), urinary outflow obstruction, nephrotoxic agents (NSAIDs, aminoglycosides), or intrinsic renal parenchymal injury.

### Blood Urea Nitrogen (BUN)
- **Typical Reference Range**: 7 - 20 mg/dL.
- **BUN/Creatinine Ratio**: An elevated ratio (>20:1) frequently points to prerenal dehydration or upper gastrointestinal bleeding.

### Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR)
- **Normal**: >= 90 mL/min/1.73m^2.
- **Mild reduction**: 60 - 89 mL/min/1.73m^2.
- **Moderate to Severe reduction**: < 60 mL/min/1.73m^2 for over 3 months signifies chronic kidney disease (CKD), requiring nephrological consultation.
