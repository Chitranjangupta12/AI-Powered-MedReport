# Endocrine: Thyroid and Glycemic Control Guidelines

**Source Organization**: American Thyroid Association (ATA) & American Diabetes Association (ADA)
**URL**: https://diabetes.org/about-diabetes/diagnosis
**Category**: Endocrinology & Metabolism
**Last Reviewed**: 2024

## Thyroid Function Tests

### Thyroid-Stimulating Hormone (TSH)
- **Role**: Pituitary hormone responding to negative feedback from circulating free thyroid hormones. Highly sensitive marker of thyroid axis status.
- **Reference Range**: Typically 0.45 - 4.5 mIU/L (may shift with pregnancy or geriatric age).
- **High TSH**: Primary Hypothyroidism (underactive thyroid) when Free T4 is low. If Free T4 is normal, it is classified as Subclinical Hypothyroidism. Common symptoms include fatigue, cold sensitivity, constipation, dry skin, and mild unexplained weight gain.
- **Low TSH**: Hyperthyroidism (overactive thyroid) when Free T4 is high, or Subclinical Hyperthyroidism if Free T4 is normal. Symptoms include palpitations, heat intolerance, tremor, insomnia, and weight loss.

### Free Thyroxine (Free T4)
- **Reference Range**: Typically 0.8 - 1.8 ng/dL. Unbound, active form of thyroxine.

## Glycemic Profile

### Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG)
- **Normal**: < 100 mg/dL (after at least 8 hours fasting).
- **Impaired Fasting Glucose (Prediabetes)**: 100 - 125 mg/dL.
- **Diabetes Threshold**: >= 126 mg/dL (requires confirmation on repeat testing).
- **Critical Emergency (>300 mg/dL or <50 mg/dL)**: Hypoglycemia (<50 mg/dL) causes neuroglycopenic symptoms (confusion, diaphoresis, seizure risk). Severe hyperglycemia (>300 mg/dL) carries risk of ketoacidosis or hyperosmolar state.

### Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c)
- **Role**: Reflects average glycemic control over the lifespan of red blood cells (~90-120 days).
- **Normal**: < 5.7%
- **Prediabetes**: 5.7% - 6.4%
- **Diabetes**: >= 6.5%
